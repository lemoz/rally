# Rally: The Thesis

## The Problem

AI today is single-player.

Millions of people sit in their own private sessions with Claude, ChatGPT, Copilot, solving the same problems independently. A developer in Berlin debugs the same auth flow a developer in Brooklyn solved yesterday. A founder in Lagos writes the same pitch deck structure a founder in London just finished. None of it compounds. None of it connects.

Meanwhile, models are getting better fast. Mythos, Opus, whatever comes next. At the limit, these models can do everything a person can do on a computer. The question stops being "can AI do this?" and becomes "who decides what AI works on?"

Right now the answer is: whoever is typing into the prompt box. One person, one session, one problem. That's an extraordinary waste of capability.

## The Insight

The most effective attention-capture mechanism ever built is the short-form video feed. TikTok proved it. Scrolller proved it. Hundreds of millions of people willingly hand over hours of their attention to these feeds every day.

But that attention goes nowhere. It's consumed and discarded. Entertainment in, dopamine out, nothing built.

The conventional response is to blame the format. "Short-form video is shallow." "Engagement metrics optimize for junk." But that misses the point. The format isn't the problem. The content is the problem. And the content is the problem because nobody has tried to make important things engaging.

People confused boring for important. They assumed that if something matters, it should feel like work to engage with. That's backwards. It's the job of the problem solvers to adapt to people, to make the things that matter as compelling as the things that don't. The feed format is a tool. Point it at something that matters and engagement becomes signal instead of waste.

## The Proposition

Rally turns engagement into alignment.

AI generates short-form videos about real projects, real problems, real progress. These videos use the styles and formats that work socially: trending aesthetics, compelling narratives, the production tricks that make people stop scrolling. But the content is about things that matter: problems being solved, projects being built, progress being made.

Users scroll. They watch, like, share, comment. That engagement becomes the signal that directs AI agent effort. The more people care about a problem, the more agent resources flow toward it. Agents work on high-signal problems, produce results, and those results generate new videos. The cycle reinforces itself.

The result: the most addictive form of entertainment becomes a force for alignment of AI agents toward things people actually care about.

## Single-Player to Multiplayer

The shift Rally enables is from single-player AI to multiplayer AI.

In single-player mode, you tell Claude what to do. Your context, your problem, your session. When you close the tab, it's gone.

In multiplayer mode, problems are shared. Agent work is visible. When one agent makes progress on a problem you care about, you see it. When you engage with a video about a project, that signal compounds with everyone else who engaged. Knowledge accumulates. Effort concentrates. Progress accelerates.

This isn't collaboration in the traditional sense: no Slack channels, no standups, no shared docs. It's collaboration through engagement. You participate by caring, visibly, and the system responds.

## Why Now

Three things are converging:

1. **Models are capable enough.** Current models can generate video, write code, analyze problems, produce plans. They're not perfect but they're good enough to be useful, and they're getting better every month.

2. **Video generation is becoming commodity.** Between Sora, Runway, Kling, Veo, and open source alternatives, generating compelling short-form video is increasingly accessible and cheap. The cost curve is falling fast.

3. **The engagement format is proven.** TikTok, Reels, Shorts, Scrolller. The UI pattern works. The engagement mechanics work. The infrastructure for building and distributing this format exists. What's missing is content worth engaging with.

## What Changes

If Rally works, the relationship between people and AI shifts fundamentally.

Today: you use AI as a tool. You direct it. You prompt it. You review its output. It's a bicycle for the mind, but you're pedaling alone.

Rally: AI becomes part of a collective. It watches what people care about. It works on those things. It reports back in a format people naturally engage with. The collective engagement of many people creates a shared direction. No one person is in charge. The signal emerges from the crowd and the agents follow it.

This isn't artificial general intelligence. It's artificial general labor, directed by collective human attention.
